The Hall of Fame exists so the people who built Oklahoma's cities and towns are remembered by the people who run them next.

How it started

The idea came from Larry Shelton, City Manager of Chickasha, and quickly earned the endorsement of the state's municipal associations. On January 19, 2000, three employees of the Oklahoma Municipal Assurance Group filed the incorporation papers with the Oklahoma Secretary of State, creating an independent, not-for-profit foundation. The first class was inducted in 2001.

Dr. Paul Lambert, then Executive Director of the Oklahoma Heritage Association, helped design the selection criteria and process the foundation still uses.

How inductees are chosen

Each year the board may induct up to six people, and one of them may be honored posthumously. Inductees are recognized at the {{ceremony}}.
